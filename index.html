The problem

Repair time is lost between diagnosis and parts availability

Workshops lose hours because parts are only sourced after diagnostics — while the bay and the technician sit blocked.

Ordered too late

Parts are ordered only after diagnostics are complete, so the wait starts when the truck is already on the lift.

Fragmented inventory

Stock is scattered across local warehouses, group stock and suppliers — with no single view of what is available when.

Manual search

Staff hunt across DMS, OEM portals, catalogs, email and phone calls to find and confirm the right part.

Automation

An AI copilot that does the parts busywork — you keep the final say

Otter Work doesn't just show you the plan. Conversational agents build the replenishment plan, draft the purchase orders and receive stock — and every money-moving action waits for a human tap to approve.

Agent · replenishment

Replenishment copilot

Ask for a demand- or usage-based plan for the location, get the key SKUs and total spend summarised, then have it draft purchase orders grouped by vendor — reviewed and released by you.

Agent · purchasing

Purchase-order copilot

List, place and receive purchase orders by reference in plain language. Placing an order with the supplier and receiving it into stock are one message away — each confirmed before it is written.

Guardrail · human-in-the-loop

Drafts, never autonomous spend

Every write is an explicit approval, never a silent purchase. Receiving ledgers each stock movement, so the next usage-based forecast only gets sharper.

Why it matters The stock manager stops assembling spreadsheets and starts approving decisions — the AI drafts, the human commits.
